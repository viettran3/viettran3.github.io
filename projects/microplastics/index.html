Microplastic-collecting underwater robot

An Arduino-based underwater robot that uses vortex formation and a plant-based solvent to capture microplastics. Award-winning at the Washington State Science & Engineering Fair.

Role
Student researcher
When
Aug 2024 – Jun 2025
Where
ASR Lab · Odessa, WA
01problem02process03final04impact

Problem

Microplastics are small, everywhere, and hard to pull out of water. I wanted to see whether a small autonomous robot could collect them, and whether a natural, non-toxic solvent could make the particles easier to catch.

Process

  • Engineered an Arduino-based underwater robot with a custom collection mechanism built around vortex formation, iterating on the prototype through repeated testing.
  • Synthesized natural deep eutectic solvents (NaDES) from decanoic acid and menthol, using controlled heating and mixing, to improve microplastic adhesion through hydrophobic interactions.
  • Ran controlled lab experiments with polyethylene microplastics, comparing solvent-treated and untreated conditions.

Final

A working prototype and an experiment showing a statistically significant improvement in particle capture with the solvent treatment (p < 0.001).

Impact

  • 2nd place, Earth & Environmental Sciences, Washington State Science & Engineering Fair (50+ projects)
  • Regional Stockholm Junior Water Prize
  • NOAA “Taking the Pulse of the Planet” award for innovation and impact
