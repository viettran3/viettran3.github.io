My personal assistant suite

A family of desktop, tablet and iPhone apps I designed and directed to run my day as a student: calendar and daily digest, meeting notes, parking alerts and a career hub.

Role
Product designer & director
When
Fall 2026 – now
Where
Desktop · Tablet · iPhone
01problem02process03final04impact

Problem

A college week is scattered across a dozen places: class calendars, course sites, club meetings, shifts, internship applications, and in Chicago, street-cleaning signs that will tow your car. Every app solved one slice and none of them talked to each other. I wanted one calm place that tells me what matters today, on whatever screen is in front of me.

Process

  • Start from my own day. I mapped a real week (classes, library shifts, deadlines, meetings) and noted every moment I had to go hunting for information.
  • One system, three screens. A desktop app for deep work, a tablet app for planning and notes, and an iPhone companion for glances and alerts, all sharing the same design language.
  • Direct, test, iterate. I wrote the specs, reviewed every build, and used the apps daily, sending changes back the same day. Dozens of versions shipped this fall.
  • Quiet by default. Notifications only when something actually needs me: a deadline tonight, a car that has to move.

Final

  • Calendar & digest: classes, events and every due date in one timeline, plus a morning digest of what's ahead.
  • Meeting notes: capture a meeting and get clean notes and action items afterwards.
  • Parking alerts: pin where I parked and get a heads-up before street cleaning or a parking restriction, with nearby safer streets.
  • Career tab: my resume versions, applications and recruiting timeline in one place.

Impact

I use the suite every day to run school, work and recruiting. Building it taught me to think in systems: how one decision ripples across three screen sizes, and how to keep a powerful tool feeling simple.
